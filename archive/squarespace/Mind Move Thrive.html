A Different Way of Holding Life

Mind Move Thrive offers therapeutic, evidence-informed support for adults and families navigating change, cultural complexity, family pressure, identity shifts, and emotional overwhelm. My work is especially shaped by themes of belonging, home, cross-cultural life, relationships, parenting, and the quiet challenge of trying to feel more grounded in yourself while life keeps moving.

ABOUT ME

My work draws on a Master’s degree in Social Work from the United States, as well as over 17 years of experience supporting children, adults, parents, and families in London, United Kingdom, across local government, charities and in private practice. It includes further training in Attachment Theory, Systemic Family Theory, mindfulness-based cognitive behavioural approaches, Polyvagal Theory, trauma-informed practice, nervous system regulation, and mind-body wellbeing. I am also a 200-hour trauma-informed yoga teacher and a certified Health Coach. I am registered with Social Work England and a member of the British Association of Social Workers (BASW).

As a New Yorker who has lived and worked across countries and cultures, including the United States, the UK, France, and Germany, I bring a global and culturally sensitive lens to my work. While the focus for people living internationally is often understandably on careers, education, school systems, language, and practical adjustment, I provide emotional and mental health support to people living outside their country of origin; raising children across cultures, religions, and languages; navigating family transitions and relationships; or trying to feel more grounded, connected, and at home in themselves and their lives.

Alongside this broader work, I also have a specialisation in providing adoption-related therapeutic support, including work around attachment, loss, identity, belonging, family relationships, parenting, complex family dynamics, and transracial or cross-cultural adoption.

MY APPROACH

Life rarely moves in straight lines. It brings twists, turns, stumbling blocks, beginnings, endings, and long stretches of in-between. My work begins with the belief that people need more than strategies in these moments. They need space to pause, notice, feel, and make sense of what is happening, while being met with care, skill, and respect for the complexity of their lives.

My approach is integrative and whole-person. It brings attention to mind, body, relationships, history, identity, and the wider contexts in which we live. It is about helping people gather the parts of themselves that may feel scattered, burdened, or pushed aside, so that life can begin to feel more connected, more meaningful, and more possible again.

My approach is shaped by professional training in mental health alongside evidence-based psychological approaches including emotional regulation strategies, attachment-informed practice, and body-based wellbeing methods.

Living and working across different countries has deeply shaped how I understand wellbeing, identity, relationships, and resilience.

I’ve experienced what it’s like to build life in new places, navigate cultures and systems, and raise a child across languages and traditions — often without familiar support networks.

This lens brings sensitivity and depth to the work I do with individuals and families navigating change in all its forms.

The focus is on practical, sustainable wellbeing that fits real life.

Parenting, Family & Teenageer Support
£0.00

Parent & Family Support — Children do not come with instructions, and family life can be complex, demanding, and emotionally full. Often, what children are asking of us lies beneath the presenting behaviour. Understanding what a child may be communicating through distress, withdrawal, anger, overwhelm, or big emotional responses is not always straightforward. Even loving, thoughtful parents can find themselves unsure how to respond in ways that help a child feel truly seen, understood, and emotionally safe. Part of this work is making sense of what may be happening underneath the surface — in your child, in the family system, and in you. It also means creating space to reflect on how you were parented, how you want to parent, and what support you may need in order to respond with greater clarity, steadiness, and self-compassion. This work may be especially helpful for adoptive parents and families seeking thoughtful, trauma-informed support informed by attachment, identity, and adoption. I also support families navigating different family structures and caregiving arrangements, as well as those adjusting to relocation, cross-cultural family life, multicultural identities, or the complexities of third culture experience.

  • Emotional regulation

  • Attachment and connection

  • Parenting under pressure

  • Cross-cultural family dynamics

  • Strengthening communication and relational safety

  • Understanding children's emotional needs and how to support them
